What it is

Every session pairs a tone with a rhythm. The tone is one sustained note. The rhythm is a slow pulse underneath it and a glowing orb on screen that swells and shrinks at the pace you should be breathing. You follow the orb, your breathing slows to meet it, and the sound moves with you. Sessions run from three minutes to ninety, and each one travels: it meets you near where you already are, takes you down or lifts you up, holds you there a while, then brings you back.

Nothing is recorded. Every tone, pulse and rainfall is built on your phone while it plays, so no two sessions are identical, nothing ever loops, and the whole app works in airplane mode. No narrator, no video, no catalogue to get through, no account, and no subscription.

The feel of it

You feel it, not just hear it.

Sound you only hear stays in your head. Aum puts weight underneath the tone and pulses your phone in time with it, so a session has a body to it.

  • Low end you can feelEvery tone carries octaves below itself, so even a high frequency sits in your chest.
  • Your phone breathes with youThe vibration motor pulses on the same rhythm as the sound, so the beat arrives through your hands as well as your ears.
  • Headphones add one moreA slow beat that isn't in either ear on its own — it appears from the difference between them.

Privacy

There is nothing to send anywhere.

Aum has no server. No account, no sign-in, no sync, no analytics, no ads, no trackers. Your session history is written to your own device and never leaves it, and you can clear it in one tap. The app requests no network permission it doesn't need and works perfectly with the radio off — which is the whole premise of synthesising everything locally. Read the full privacy policy.

Important

Aum plays sound and vibration. It is not a medical device and does not treat anything.

If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, talk to a doctor first. Every session uses rhythmic stimulation.

Don't use it while driving — the sleep programs work. Check with a clinician first if you're pregnant, have a pacemaker, or are being treated for a heart, neurological or psychiatric condition. Keep the volume low.
