How it works

Three layers, generated live.

Each one is doing a different job, and each one is honest about how well supported it is.

Frequency

The carrier. Real-time additive synthesis — ten partials in a three-voice detuned ensemble that drifts in pitch and level, plus sub-octaves, so even a 963 Hz session still puts energy in your chest.

Vibration

Two kinds. Acoustic: binaural beating for headphones, isochronic gating for speakers, from 2 Hz delta to 40 Hz gamma. Physical: the phone's own motor, driven in time with the breath.

Breath

Every session swells to a pattern you choose, with a visual guide reading from the same phase list that shapes the sound. This is the best-evidenced layer in the app, and it runs under everything else.

Researched

Nothing is resampled and nothing is sampled. The app ships no audio assets at all — every layer is generated at its target frequency, which is why a 963 Hz session doesn't arrive as metallic hiss.

Breath

The pattern shapes the sound, not just the guide.

A pattern is an ordered phase list, so the envelope, the visual guide, the haptic ticks and the stereo cue all generate from one definition. The swell follows your breath. Holds go still, so you can feel exactly where you are in the cycle.

  • Ten patterns, plus one you buildCoherent, Deep Coherent, Physiological Sigh, 4·7·8, Box, Extended Exhale, Energizing, Triangle, Alternate Nostril, and a Descending pace that slows across the session.
  • Holds get their own cueA draining ring and a countdown, because a flat envelope can't tell you whether you're holding or exhaling very slowly — and people lose the pattern within two cycles without one.
  • Alternate Nostril actually pansThe sound moves to the side you're breathing through.
  • Your own pattern is rated honestlyCustom is four numbers — in, hold, out, rest. Dial in 5 and 5 and it says researched, because at six breaths a minute you've built resonance breathing whether or not you knew that's what it was called.

Around the tone

Nothing here ever loops.

Rain, ocean, wind, leaves and birdsong — all synthesised, none sampled. Non-repetition comes from layering coprime buffer lengths: two birdsong flocks at 37 and 43 seconds realign every 26 minutes, drifting over gust modulation on a third period. It's the tell that gives every other sleep app away around minute forty.

  • Birdsong that reads as birdsA chirp is a frequency sweep, so each call integrates its contour into phase. Calls come in phrases of two to five notes sharing a pan position and a loudness — because that's one bird in one tree. Evenly spaced chirps read as a machine instantly.
  • A bell in four charactersBowl, Bell, Chime and Gong, distinguished by partial structure rather than pitch. Tuned to whatever the session is playing, so a gong tracking 963 Hz rings at 120 Hz while a bowl rings at 241.
  • Presence, from distant to directFive steps that sweep the tone into a resonant noise bed — pink noise through high-Q filters tuned to the carrier. A spectrum analyser sees the same peak; the ear hears air. The sub-octaves hold their level throughout, so distant never means quieter.

Honesty

The screen most apps like this don't ship.

Aum layers several different things on top of each other, and they are not equally well supported. Every layer, every tone and every breathing pattern carries an evidence tier you can read in the app — and the tradition's own claims are quoted, attributed and corrected in the same breath rather than quietly deleted.

Slow breathing at six a minute

Researched

Hits the resonance frequency of the baroreflex loop and maximises heart-rate variability. Trials of paced breathing — the practice, not this app — have repeatedly reported reductions in anxiety and blood pressure. It runs under every session here.

Rhythmic stimulation

Emerging

The matching steady-state response in EEG is solid and easy to measure. Whether it reliably changes how you feel is less certain, and the subjective effect is smaller and more variable than the marketing suggests.

Vibration you can feel

Emerging

Vibroacoustic therapy has moderate evidence behind it. A phone's vibration motor is a modest version of that, and the app says so.

The specific Solfeggio frequencies

Traditional

No clinical evidence for the individual claims. 528 Hz does not repair DNA — that claim traces to a 1990s book, not to any laboratory result. They're here because the tradition is coherent and a deliberately chosen tone holds attention better than an arbitrary one.

Privacy

There is nothing to send anywhere.

Aum has no server. No account, no sign-in, no sync, no analytics, no ads, no trackers. Your session history is written to your own device and never leaves it, and you can clear it in one tap. The app requests no network permission it doesn't need and works perfectly with the radio off — which is the whole premise of synthesising everything locally. Read the full privacy policy.

Important — please read

Aum is not a medical device and does not treat, diagnose, cure or prevent anything. It plays sound and vibration. Nothing in the app or on this page is medical advice, and no frequency here has been shown to produce a clinical effect on a body.

If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, talk to a doctor before using rhythmic audio stimulation. Rhythmic stimulation is the one part of this app with a known contraindication, and it is present in every session.

Don't use Aum while driving or operating machinery — the sleep programs are built to make you drowsy and they work. If you're pregnant, have a pacemaker or another implanted device, or are being treated for a heart, neurological or psychiatric condition, check with a clinician first. Stop if anything is uncomfortable, and keep the volume low: prolonged listening at high volume damages hearing regardless of what frequency is playing.
